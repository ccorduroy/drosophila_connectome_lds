PART 3: does the data look like what a linear-Gaussian state-space model assumes?
==============================================================================
  3a. flat high-frequency floor (0.45-0.59 Hz) = 0.47 of total variance on average (per fly 0.32 to 0.60)
      -> imaging noise is a large share of every frame, so a model with an explicit observation-noise term
         is justified, and one-step R^2 against noisy frames is capped near 1 - 0.47 = 0.53.
      (assumes the floor extends flat below 0.45 Hz; some of it could be real fast activity, so treat as approximate)
  3b. residual lag-1 autocorrelation: median -0.01 (range -0.10 to +0.17) -> residuals are close to white, so one lag of memory captures the linear temporal structure
      residual excess kurtosis (0 = Gaussian): median 0.61 (range 0.27 to 2.53) -> mildly heavy-tailed; roughly Gaussian for most flies, with a few outlier-prone ones
      residual cross-region |correlation| (0 = independent): median 0.16 -> residuals are shared across regions, so the noise covariance should be FULL, not diagonal
      sanity check on 3a: flies with a higher noise floor are harder to predict (Spearman rho = -0.80 between floor and LOFO R^2)
  3c. leave-one-fly-out median R^2 by model:  each region alone (diagonal A) 0.274 | full VAR(1) 0.341 | VAR(2) 0.350 | VAR(3) 0.350 | VAR(5) 0.350
      -> cross-region coupling adds +0.067 R^2 over regions-alone; extra lags add at most +0.010,
         so a first-order model on the 36 regions is adequate (the data do not demand extra memory/hidden states)
  3d. PCA of the 36 regions: PC1 = 33%, first 3 = 45%, first 5 = 52% of variance; participation ratio = 7.8; 90% needs 25 PCs
      (a few strong shared modes plus a long flat tail, consistent with the noise floor in 3a: real redundancy to exploit)
  3e. FC pattern, first half vs second half of each recording: rho median 0.87 (range 0.75 to 0.95); same-fly 70/30 R^2 (0.342) ~ cross-fly R^2 (0.341)
      -> dynamics are stable within a recording and shared across animals, which is what a fixed A requires

PART 4: what does the connectome add beyond generic structure?
==============================================================================
  4a. off-diagonal of the fitted A (symmetrised) vs wiring: Spearman rho = 0.380, permutation p = 0.0005
      -> the dynamics, not just the correlations, line up with the connectome (weaker than FC, as expected: A is a harder object)
  4b. small-data learning curve (median R^2 on the 15 test flies; train on the first m frames of the SAME fly only)
      m frames              ridge to zero       persistence prior  uniform-coupling prior        connectome prior   shuffled-wiring prior
            30                      0.162                   0.255                   0.280                   0.288                   0.280
            60                      0.196                   0.262                   0.281                   0.290                   0.281
           120                      0.239                   0.291                   0.298                   0.300                   0.298
           250                      0.265                   0.306                   0.304                   0.310                   0.304
           500                      0.311                   0.339                   0.339                   0.340                   0.339
          1000                      0.343                   0.348                   0.348                   0.348                   0.348
      m=30 (25 s of data): connectome - shuffled = +0.008 median over flies (connectome better in 14/15 flies, Wilcoxon p = 0.0001)
      m=120 (100 s of data): connectome - shuffled = -0.003 median over flies (connectome better in 7/15 flies, Wilcoxon p = 0.7197)
      -> nearly all of the small-data gain comes from a persistence + shared-coupling prior (which a shuffled matrix also supplies).
         The specific wiring adds only about +0.01 R^2, visible at ~30-60 frames and gone by ~120 frames: at region level,
         a connectome prior on A is barely separable from generic shrinkage.
  4c. hidden-region reconstruction, median R^2 on hidden regions (10 random hidden sets x 20 held-out flies)
      hidden    static-conditioning   +temporal context   wiring-only   shuffled-wiring   uniform-average
        25%                   0.410               0.427           0.279           0.268           0.268
        50%                   0.365               0.382           0.266           0.246           0.249
        75%                   0.311               0.330           0.239           0.220           0.224
      25% hidden: wiring - shuffled = +0.017 (better in 20/20 flies, Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-06);   context - static = +0.019 (better in 19/20)
      50% hidden: wiring - shuffled = +0.017 (better in 20/20 flies, Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-06);   context - static = +0.019 (better in 19/20)
      75% hidden: wiring - shuffled = +0.016 (better in 20/20 flies, Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-06);   context - static = +0.023 (better in 19/20)
      -> wiring-only beats a shuffled or uniform guess by a small but consistent margin (about +0.02 R^2) without seeing any
         data from the hidden region; the covariance learned from other flies does far better (it knows the hidden region);
         adding +/-2 frames of temporal context (the part a smoother adds) gains only about +0.02 on top of that.
